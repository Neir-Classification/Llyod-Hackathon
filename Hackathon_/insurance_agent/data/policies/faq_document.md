## Water Damage Questions

### Q: Does my policy cover water damage from a burst pipe?
**A:** Yes. Water damage from a burst pipe inside your home is covered under "Accidental Discharge of Water or Steam" (Peril 12). This includes damage to floors, walls, and personal property. However, the pipe itself is typically not covered - only the resulting water damage.

**Reference:** Section I - Perils Insured Against, Item 12

---

### Q: My sump pump failed and my basement flooded. Is this covered?
**A:** Only if you have the **Water Backup Endorsement (HO-04-95)**. Standard homeowners policies specifically EXCLUDE water damage from sump pump failure or overflow.

If you have the endorsement:
- Coverage up to your endorsement limit ($5,000 - $25,000 typical)
- Deductible applies
- Covers damage to basement contents and structure

If you do NOT have the endorsement:
- This damage is NOT covered
- You may want to add the endorsement to prevent future losses

**Reference:** Section I - Exclusions, Item C (Water Damage); Water Backup Endorsement HO-04-95

---

### Q: The river near my house flooded and damaged my basement. Is this covered?
**A:** No. **Flood damage from rivers, lakes, streams, or any natural body of water is NEVER covered by homeowners insurance.** This includes:
- River overflow
- Storm surge
- Tidal water
- Tsunamis
- Rising groundwater

To get flood coverage, you must purchase a separate flood insurance policy through:
- National Flood Insurance Program (NFIP)
- Private flood insurers

**Reference:** Section I - Exclusions, Item C (Water Damage), Subsection 1

---

### Q: My neighbor's pipe burst and flooded my basement through the shared wall. Who pays?
**A:** You would file a claim on YOUR homeowners policy. Water damage from a burst pipe is a covered peril, regardless of whose pipe burst. Your insurance will pay for your damages (minus deductible) and may subrogate against your neighbor's insurance to recover costs.

**Reference:** Section I - Perils Insured Against, Item 12; Conditions - Subrogation

## Mold Questions

### Q: I found mold behind my washing machine. Is that covered?
**A:** It depends on the cause and when you found it.

**COVERED (up to $5,000) if:**
1. Mold resulted from sudden, accidental water damage
2. The damage was hidden from view
3. You report it within 14 days of discovery
4. You take steps to prevent further damage

**NOT COVERED if:**
- Mold is due to humidity, condensation, or poor ventilation
- Mold was caused by a flood
- You knew about water intrusion but didn't address it
- More than 14 days passed since discovery without reporting

**Reference:** Special Exclusions - Mold, Fungus, and Wet or Dry Rot; Exception - Hidden Water Damage Mold Coverage

## Rental & Business Use Questions

### Q: I'm renting my house out on Airbnb for the weekend. Does my standard home insurance cover theft by a guest?
**A:** **No.** Standard homeowners insurance specifically excludes coverage when you rent your home to others, even occasionally.

When you rent your home (short-term rentals like Airbnb, VRBO):
- ❌ Theft by guests is NOT covered
- ❌ Property damage by guests is NOT covered  
- ❌ Liability for guest injuries is NOT covered

**To be properly covered, you must:**
1. Notify your insurance company about rental activity
2. Purchase landlord/rental dwelling insurance, OR
3. Add a Short-Term Rental Endorsement (if available)

**Reference:** Special Exclusions - Business Use Exclusion; Short-Term Rental Specific Exclusion

---

### Q: I run a small business from my home. Am I covered?
**A:** Standard homeowners policies have LIMITED coverage for home businesses:
- Business property on premises: $2,500 limit
- Business property off premises: $500 limit
- NO liability coverage for business activities

**For proper coverage, add the Home Business Endorsement** which provides:
- Increased business property limits
- Business liability coverage
- Coverage for business equipment

**Reference:** Coverage C - Special Limits of Liability; Section II - Exclusions (Business Activities)

## Tree & Outdoor Structure Questions

### Q: A tree fell on my fence, but the tree belongs to the city. Who pays?
**A:** In most cases, **YOU file a claim on YOUR homeowners policy.** 

Here's how tree damage liability works:
- **Your fence is covered** under Coverage B (Other Structures) if the tree fell due to a covered peril (wind, ice, etc.)
- **The city is typically NOT liable** unless they were notified the tree was dangerous and failed to act
- Coverage is limited to 5% of Coverage A for trees/shrubs/landscaping
- Single tree maximum: $500

**You may be able to recover costs from the city if:**
- You previously reported the tree as a hazard
- The tree was visibly dead or diseased
- The city acknowledged the danger but didn't act

**Reference:** Coverage B - Other Structures; Tree, Shrub, and Plant Coverage

---

### Q: My neighbor's tree fell on my garage. Is my neighbor responsible?
**A:** Generally, **no** - you file on YOUR insurance. Your neighbor is only liable if:
- The tree was clearly dead or diseased, AND
- They knew about it or were notified, AND
- They failed to take reasonable action

In normal circumstances (healthy tree felled by storm):
- File claim on YOUR policy
- Your Coverage B covers the garage
- Your Coverage A may cover attached structures
- Your insurance may subrogate if negligence can be proven

**Reference:** Tree Damage Liability Section; Coverage B - Other Structures

## Jewelry & Valuables Questions

### Q: I just bought a $10,000 engagement ring. Is it covered under my "contents" limit?
**A:** **Partially, but probably not fully.** Standard homeowners policies have special limits for jewelry:

- **Standard jewelry limit: $1,500 TOTAL** for all jewelry, watches, and furs
- This means your $10,000 ring would only be covered up to $1,500

**To fully protect valuable jewelry:**
1. Add a **Scheduled Personal Property Endorsement** (jewelry rider/floater)
2. Get the item appraised
3. Submit appraisal to your insurer
4. Pay additional premium (typically $1-2 per $100 of value annually)

**Benefits of scheduling jewelry:**
- Full appraised value coverage
- No deductible typically
- Broader coverage (including mysterious disappearance)
- Worldwide protection

**Reference:** Coverage C - Special Limits of Liability; Endorsements Available - Scheduled Personal Property

---

### Q: What's the difference between "actual cash value" and "replacement cost"?
**A:** 
- **Actual Cash Value (ACV):** Replacement cost MINUS depreciation. If your 5-year-old TV is destroyed, you get the current value of a 5-year-old TV.
- **Replacement Cost:** The cost to replace with new, similar item. No deduction for age or wear.

Standard policies cover personal property at ACV. To get replacement cost, add the **Replacement Cost Coverage endorsement**.

**Reference:** Conditions - Loss Settlement

## Claims Questions

### Q: How long do I have to report a claim?
**A:** Report losses **as soon as practicable** (immediately if possible). Specific deadlines:
- Report loss: Immediately/as soon as possible
- Submit proof of loss: Within 60 days
- File lawsuit: Within 2 years of loss date

Delays in reporting can result in claim denial, especially for time-sensitive issues like water damage or mold.

**Reference:** Conditions - Duties After Loss; Your Duties After Loss - Time Requirements

---

### Q: What should I do immediately after damage occurs?
**A:** 
1. **Ensure safety first** - evacuate if necessary
2. **Prevent further damage** - cover broken windows, stop water flow, etc.
3. **Document everything** - photos, videos, lists
4. **Contact your insurer** - report the loss promptly
5. **Don't throw anything away** - keep damaged items for adjuster
6. **Save receipts** - for any emergency repairs or expenses

**Reference:** Conditions - Duties After Loss

## Coverage Limit Questions

### Q: What is my deductible and how does it work?
**A:** Your deductible is the amount YOU pay before insurance kicks in. It's shown in your Declarations page.

**Example:** If you have $5,000 in covered damage and a $1,000 deductible:
- You pay: $1,000
- Insurance pays: $4,000

Deductibles apply per occurrence, not per item damaged.

---

### Q: Can I make a claim for damage less than my deductible?
**A:** Technically yes, but it's usually not advisable. If damage is less than your deductible, you'll receive no payment. Additionally, filing claims (even denied ones) can affect your insurance record.

**Reference:** Understanding your deductible in Declarations
